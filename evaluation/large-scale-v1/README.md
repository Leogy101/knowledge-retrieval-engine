# Frozen synthetic retrieval benchmark

This fixture contains 72 authored synthetic documents and 24 difficult tasks. It is preparation for a separately authorized live comparison, not a record of model results. Corpus authoring did not inspect or evaluate the KRE retrieval implementation. The author validated source facts and exact spans before handing the frozen task set to the benchmark runner.

The complete corpus is 463,199 UTF-8 bytes, approximately 68,022 whitespace-delimited words. Six long technical manuals are approximately 69 KB each. Fictional Harbor and Meridian projects contain no real people, private workspace material, credentials or actual service endpoints. All newly authored fixture documents and task text are dedicated to CC0-1.0. Existing software licensing remains unchanged.

| large | 60 | 457,462 | 18 | Independently defined non-KRE retrieval under matched context limits |

These are raw source sizes, not model token counts or a measured tokenizer capacity test. Every task grants access to its entire declared collection. Lifecycle eligibility and source labels must be identical between arms; no task-specific gold-document shortlist is allowed. Report the two baseline strata separately.

There are three tasks in each category: multi-document questions, three-or-more-passage questions, conflicting documentation, superseded instructions, buried manual details, insufficient evidence, code/configuration interpretation, and checkpoint continuation. The fixture includes 67 required facts and 63 required evidence occurrences across 54 distinct spans. Three tasks require `insufficient_evidence`. Several active unverified documents contradict governing authoritative records. Timestamps do not decide authority. Three superseded and three revoked source snapshots remain recorded for lifecycle checks, not current-answer retrieval. Two stale documents remain excluded current-answer negatives, and two unknown-freshness documents retain explicit uncertainty labels; two records are explicitly derived/model-inference assertions.

The three checkpoint tasks have a deterministic three-stage setup: capture the frozen card while its referenced source is active; apply the named revocation or supersession; resume against the final corpus. Both arms receive equivalent task information and preserved user constraints. They use one answer-generation call each. This tests continuation after deterministic reference validation, not model-generated compaction, a multi-turn conversation or independent model memory. Do not present it as evidence for those broader behaviors.

`corpus.json` stores document identity, immutable path/revision/hash, collection scope and policy labels. `tasks.json` contains questions, reference answers, fact expectations, exact gold evidence spans, forbidden claims, constraints and checkpoint setup. `freeze.json` hashes the corpus/task manifests, source text and deterministic generator. `gold-validation.json` records the author-side exact-span and lifecycle-reference checks. Gold answers, required spans and forbidden-claim oracles are evaluation-only; none may be inserted into the inference prompt or used for candidate selection.

`build_fixture.py` reproduces this authored fixture without importing KRE, accessing a network or invoking a model. It is an authoring tool, not permission to mutate the benchmark after observing results. Any later correction must preserve the prior freeze and receive a new version before new evaluation. Protocol files may be frozen separately by the runner; they are not part of the source/task authoring freeze.

This is a deliberately challenging synthetic sample, not a randomly sampled collection of public projects or independently human-authored field data. Long manuals repeat operational cautions and handoff patterns while varying subsystem details. That repetition is a disclosed realism limitation; it can affect lexical retrieval and should not be hidden when reporting results. The benchmark must retain all failures and must not tune questions, prompts or retrieval settings after seeing arm results.

Known equivalent evidence is frozen in `evidence_groups` for LS05, LS15 and LS21. Each group accepts any listed alternative; each alternative requires all of its exact spans. Canonical gold-span recall remains a separate descriptive metric. The short export policy is valid evidence for recall limits even when the long manual was the author’s reference. Parser answers may be supported by the documented code rather than requiring redundant worked-example excerpts. These alternatives are authored before retrieval results and are evaluation-only. They are not an exhaustive semantic-equivalence engine; independent review must identify legitimate evidence that the enumerated oracle missed.
